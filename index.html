      How it's calculated & settings

      Each meal's offset is the welfare gap: the extra you would have paid if the meat had come from animals raised and slaughtered to high welfare standards (e.g. slower-growing breeds, outdoor or pasture access, lower stocking densities, effective stunning).

      offset = price paid × meat share × (humane multiplier − 1)

      Humane multiplier is how many times more the high-welfare version costs than ordinary supermarket meat. Where data exists it compares USDA's 2026 averages for pasture-raised / grass-fed meat sold directly by farms with the BLS average supermarket price for the same product. That's what humane meat actually costs to buy today. Farm-direct prices include some small-farm overhead that wouldn't exist at scale; industry cost studies of cheaper welfare reforms show much smaller premiums (see chicken). Meats marked estimate have no good price data, and the reasoning is given instead.

      Meat share is how much of what you paid went on the meat. Restaurants spend about 28–35% of the menu price on all ingredients, and the meat is usually the biggest part of that, so about 20% of a restaurant dish's price is the meat. A subsidized work cafeteria charges roughly the ingredient cost, and on that basis the meat is about two-thirds of the ingredients (20% of a menu price out of the ~30% spent on ingredients), so the cafeteria default is 65%. A grocery purchase is nearly all meat.

      Note: this measures the price gap, not suffering. Cheap fixes like stunning fish and shrimp barely change the price, so those offsets come out small even though each dollar of fish or shrimp involves many animals.

      Edits apply to new entries only. Logged meals keep the numbers they were logged with.
